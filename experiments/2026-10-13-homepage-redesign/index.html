I am a Computational Scientist at the Research Computing Center, University of Chicago, where I work with research groups on environmental science, GeoAI and scalable geospatial computing. Before that I held postdoctoral positions at the University of Wisconsin–Madison and the University of Arizona, and I earned my Ph.D. at Boise State University. I have contributed to NASA programs including Surface Biology and Geology, PACE and Drylands, and have secured and contributed to over $1M in research funding.

Research

Disturbance and recovery

How ecosystems respond to fire, land-cover change and climate variability, and what “recovery” means when the baseline itself is shifting: did an ecosystem return to what it was, or to what it could have been?

Key papers: GRL 2024 · ERL 2022

Earth system modeling and data assimilation

Bringing Earth observations into process models (CESM/CLM, ED2, TECs, GCAM) with data assimilation (DART), to constrain the carbon cycle, surface energy balance and land–climate feedbacks, from the Arctic–Boreal region to global drylands.

Key papers: AFM 2021 · JGR Biogeosciences 2024

Remote sensing of vegetation function

Leaf traits from hyperspectral data, solar-induced fluorescence and lidar, across ground, airborne and spaceborne sensors (GEDI, PRISMA, NEON AOP). Using 400+ eddy-covariance sites, I showed that canopy structure shapes maximum light-use efficiency nonlinearly across biomes.

Key paper: Ecology Letters 2025

Ecosystems, food systems and policy

How climate-driven changes in ecosystem productivity restructure food production, and what that means for climate-mitigation policy.

Preprint: bioRxiv 2025, submitted to One Earth
