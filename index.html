About Me

I am a geospatial computational scientist working at the intersection of remote sensing, Earth system modeling, and data science. I am currently a Geospatial Computational Scientist at the Research Computing Center, University of Chicago, where I am the lead computational scientist on three funded projects spanning environmental science, biomedicine, and human–environment interactions. Previously, I was a postdoctoral research associate at the University of Wisconsin–Madison (2021–2025) and the University of Arizona (2019–2021), and I earned my Ph.D. in Geosciences at Boise State University.

My work spans from leaf-level photosynthesis to global carbon and food systems, always asking how ecosystems are connected with Earth and human systems. I contributed to algorithm development for NASA's Surface Biology and Geology mission, conceived and wrote a funded NASA ROSES proposal ($830K) on dryland ephemeral vegetation using PACE hyperspectral imaging, and have secured and contributed to over $1M in funded research.

At UChicago, I established a nudged CESM2 modeling capability on the university's HPC, am developing retrieval methods for NASA PACE hyperspectral imagery, and lead the geospatial AI component of a university initiative on AI-driven discovery in human–environment interaction. I have designed and taught workshops on Earth system modeling and scalable geospatial analysis, and I founded and advised the UChicago Space Program TORCH student team, runner-up in the NASA Space to Soil Challenge. I am passionate about open science, reproducibility, and mentoring the next generation of scientists in environmental, computational, and geospatial sciences.

Research

Vegetation, Disturbance & Ecosystem Recovery
I study how ecosystems respond to disturbance — fire, land cover change, climate variability — and what "recovery" means when ecosystems are non-stationary. I introduced the concept of counterfactual recovery, showing that Arctic–boreal disturbance recovery is misjudged when measured against pre-disturbance conditions rather than undisturbed trajectories (GRL, 2024; among the journal's most-read papers of 2025). Earlier, I showed that conventional approaches underestimate how land cover change alters surface energy and temperature across the Arctic–boreal region of North America (ERL, 2022).

Earth System Modeling & Data Assimilation
I integrate Earth observations into process-based models (CESM/CLM, E3SM, ED2, SiB, GCAM) to improve our understanding of the carbon cycle, surface energy balance, and land–atmosphere interactions. I have contributed to assimilating satellite leaf area and biomass observations into CLM5, constraining simulated carbon uptake and land surface energy exchange globally and in the Arctic–boreal region (co-authored, JGR Biogeosciences, 2022, 2024), and I parameterized and evaluated the Ecosystem Demography model for sagebrush drylands (GMD, 2019; AFM, 2021).

Remote Sensing & Vegetation Physiology
From hyperspectral leaf trait retrieval to global Solar-Induced Fluorescence (SIF) from OCO-2, I develop and apply remote sensing methods to track vegetation function at scale. I work with the full observational stack: ground-based spectrometers, TLS, UAVs, airborne campaigns including NEON AOP, and spaceborne sensors including GEDI, PRISMA, and PACE. In a recent study (Ecology Letters, 2025), I combined data from over 400 eddy covariance sites with satellite observations to show that canopy structure governs biome-level maximum light use efficiency — a fundamental, intrinsic measure of a plant's capacity to convert light into carbon — through both linear and nonlinear pathways. Earlier work combined lidar and hyperspectral data to map dryland vegetation (Remote Sensing, 2019) and showed that empirical nitrogen retrieval in drylands yields unreliable inferences about ecosystem function (IEEE TGRS, 2019).

Vegetation, Food Systems & Climate Policy
A growing thread of my work connects Earth system processes to human systems. By coupling an integrated assessment model with an Earth system model, I examined how changes in global ecosystem productivity propagate through food systems beyond crops (preprint, bioRxiv, 2025). I also secured a $150K postdoctoral fellowship to couple GCAM with Earth system models and assess carbon mitigation pathways for the US dairy sector.
